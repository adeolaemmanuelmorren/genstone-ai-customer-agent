## Repeated Caller Questions

- What product, profile, color, component, package quantity, price unit, and square-foot coverage am I buying?
- How many panels, outside corners, inside corners, ledgers, caps, adhesive tubes, sealant tubes, and touch-up materials does this measured project require?
- Where can I see samples or a display locally, and can a store, contractor, designer, or wholesale branch obtain samples and literature?
- Does GenStone sell or arrange installation, and does it serve the caller's state or country?
- How should a fireplace, chimney, column, pillar, retaining-wall face, window/door edge, or freestanding corner feature be designed and finished?
- Did my order go through; what has shipped; which carrier has it; what does each tracking number cover; and when will it arrive?
- How do I report damage, a shortage, a wrong color/item, an address problem, or a suspected delivery to the wrong place?
- Can I cancel, change, return, exchange, or reorder material, and when will a label, replacement, credit, or refund be issued?
- How do a promotion, discount code, free-shipping offer, marketplace listing, or retail-channel price apply?
- How do I use the visualizer, submit photos, receive a rendering, or match an older/existing installation?

## Caller Terminology

- Callers commonly said "sheets," "4x8 panels," "vinyl brick siding," "flexible stone," "real stone," "filler panels," "planks," "brick ledge," "outside corner ledger," and "pillar coverage" when the precise GenStone component was uncertain.
- "Ledger" was used for both horizontal ledger trim and, incorrectly, large areas of wall panel coverage.
- "Retail partner," "retail customer," "customer," "builder," "contractor," and "wholesaler" were routinely confused by both callers and intake agents.
- "Order number," "PO," "PRO number," "pickup number," tracking number, package/master tracking, and booking ID were not consistently understood.
- Product names were often approximated or misheard, including Northern Slate/Northern Lights, Desert Sunrise, Vanilla Bean, Keystone, Kenai, Iron Ore, Deep Red, Multi Color, Arctic Smoke, Stratford, and River Rock/Riverwalk.

## Repeated Misunderstandings

- A displayed price was repeatedly assumed to cover a box, a four-sided post kit, a cap, or other bundle when it may have represented one component.
- Customers repeatedly expected panels to be 2x4 or 4x8 sheets and could not find actual dimensions, coverage, or package counts.
- Fireplace and lifestyle imagery led some callers to think GenStone sold the complete fireplace, fireproofing system, steel shell, or installation labor.
- Some callers believed paint, caulk, adhesive, tutorials, or other accessories were automatically included; the transcripts do not establish that these were part of the purchased SKUs.
- A business address or phone number on carrier labels was mistaken for the recipient's contact information, contributing to delivery failures.
- Website photos were treated as reliable color matches even when physical samples or delivered material looked different.
- "Ground" shipping, sale deadlines, callbacks, and visualizer submissions were often interpreted as promises with a known turnaround even when none was stated clearly.
- Decorative veneer was sometimes proposed as structural waterproofing, retaining-wall protection, or listed fire protection; those functions must remain separate.

## Common Agent Explanations

- The answering service usually collected identity/contact details, classified the caller, and scheduled or requested a project-coordinator callback.
- Answering agents commonly said they lacked access to order history, carrier details, pricing, inventory, product specifications, returns, claims, or named-employee schedules.
- Business hours were generally described as Monday through Friday, 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM Mountain Time.
- Callers were often told a coordinator could discuss pricing, quantities, samples, promotions, installation, or an assisted order.
- These explanations are observed intake behavior, not approved policy; several agents guessed about locations, installers, retail relationships, included accessories, or callback timing.

## Missing Knowledge and Possible Website Conflicts

- Unit-of-sale, pieces per package, current price basis, per-panel coverage, accessory compatibility, and whether caps/paint/caulk/adhesive are included need SKU-level answers.
- A visual component guide is needed for panels, half panels, outside/inside corners, fingered corners, ledgers, ledger corners, caps, trim, paint, caulk, and adhesive.
- The website's project imagery and purchasing flow do not adequately explain appliance exclusions, installation-labor boundaries, display versus special-order stores, distributor channels, or complete fireplace-system safety requirements.
- Repeated customer assertions that instructions promise included paint/caulk conflict with the lack of a verified universal inclusion rule.
- Public shipping language does not resolve processing versus transit time, split shipments, carrier selection, expedited options, sample timing, or item-level tracking.
- Public returns/claims material needs clearer workflows for partial overage, wrong item/color, cancellation, marketplace/retail purchases, damage evidence, retaining packaging, labels, RGA, credits, and case status.
- Custom-color manufacture/finish, custom caps, warranty registration, repainting/sealing older material, exact composition/country of origin, fire-test documentation, and international availability remain unresolved or require authoritative technical sources.
- Visualizer upload confirmation, turnaround, free versus paid/custom rendering, and failed-submission recovery remain unclear.

## Operational Patterns

- Order-status opacity was the most persistent service problem: missing confirmations, no item-level shipment state, multiple packages under incomplete tracking, overdue ETAs, and unrecognized carrier contacts.
- Carrier labels repeatedly used an incorrect/incomplete address or the GenStone business number instead of the recipient's number; multiple independent drivers called because they could not complete delivery.
- Checkout failures recurred across devices and payment methods, including inert card controls, rejected addresses, promotion-form validation, a required merchant-defined field, and "null is not an object."
- Claims and returns lacked accessible evidence upload, receipt/acknowledgment, case number, next-response target, printable/non-print return-label options, and clear retain/dispose instructions.
- Scheduled callbacks were missed, time-zone controls confused agents, caller ID was uncertain, and repeated intake discouraged callers with simple or urgent questions.
- Retail/wholesale staff need a dedicated path for pricing, inventory, samples, literature, field representatives, distributor lookup, PO tracking, RGA, credit, and disposition.
- Sales estimates repeatedly omitted or undercounted panels/corners or did not expose waste/layout assumptions, sometimes stopping on-site labor.
- Confirmation records that omitted itemization or address details undermined trust and, in one case, caused repeated fraud concern.
- Do-not-call and marketing opt-out requests are compliance operations and should remain outside the customer knowledge draft.
