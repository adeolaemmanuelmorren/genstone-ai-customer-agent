### Life Tested

#### How durable is GenStone?

- Article ID: 360053604473
- Status: Published
- Visibility: Restricted
- Locale: en-us
- Updated: 2020-08-27T18:47:30Z
- Zendesk URL: https://support.genstone.com/hc/en-us/articles/360053604473

GenStone is extremely durable and to prove this we put the stone veneer to the test. We used various testing methods to show and compare the durability of GenStone vs natural stone. Below are the following tests we executed, along with a brief summary of the results. There are also videos of each test we did which you can view in our article, ***["Overcoming the Shortcomings of Other Faux Stone".](https://go.genstone.com/comparisons-lifetesting-durability)***

**Sledgehammer Test:** The head of a sledgehammer usually weighs up to 20 pounds and lands with 1,000 pounds of impact force. GenStone panel withstands the force without cracking or breaking while the natural stone suffered substantial damage.

**Gravity Test: **We first tested each product – a GenStone panel and a piece of natural stone – by dropping them approximately 4 feet from the ground. The piece of natural stone displayed its fragile nature, cracking, and breaking upon impact. The GenStone panel remained unblemished. 

**Jeep Rubicon Test: **Our most demanding test yet put the stone solutions against a Jeep Rubicon that weighs more than 4,000 pounds. Our lightweight, 11-pound panel once again proved superior to the elements, easily withstanding the weight and force of the Jeep.

#### What maintenance do you recommend?

- Article ID: 360051822574
- Status: Published
- Visibility: Restricted
- Locale: en-us
- Updated: 2020-08-03T22:54:06Z
- Zendesk URL: https://support.genstone.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051822574

GenStone products need very little maintenance which is why it is such a popular product. In most cases, normal rainfall is sufficient to keep it clean, however, if you want or need to deep clean the faux stone, I have listed below some suggestions to help with up-keep. 

***[Removing Dirt & Grime](https://go.genstone.com/upkeep-advice-for-maintenance)***

*Click the heading above to be directed to our maintenance page*

**Moderate Dirt:** We recommend an occasional washing with clear water using a garden hose and soft-bristled brush (a long-handled, car-washing brush is ideal for this purpose).

**Deeper Clean: **For a deeper clean, refer to the suggestion above but use the following solution for cleaner.

- * 1/3 cup detergent or 1/3 cup dish soap (Tide®, for example)*

- * 2/3 cup trisodium phosphate (Soilmax or Dirtex, for example)*

- * 1-gallon water*

**Mildew: **Mildew can collect on surfaces of all types of building products and is often evident on surfaces that have not been properly cared for and maintained. Normally, mildew will appear as black spots. Mildew is easy to remove by using the basic cleaning solution above with the addition of sodium hypochlorite as follows:

- * 1/3 cup detergent (Tide®, for example)*

- * 2/3 cup trisodium phosphate (Soilax®, for example)*

- * 1-gallon water*

**Other General Recommendations: **Like all other siding products, exposure to the elements may cause gradual, uniform change over time. At the first sign of any undesirable change in the appearance of your GenStone, we recommend applying Anvil Aqua Seal immediately and as required in the future. Applying the Aqua Seal will bring the panels back to their original look and color concentrations. Before applying the Aqua Seal, we recommend touching up areas where the paint may have come off the panels. Flaking paint is very uncommon and usually a result of the homeowner cleaning the GenStone with a high powered stream of water or harsh environmental factors.

**Anvil Aqua Seal or Similar Product:** Typically covers 80-300 SQFT per gallon depending on surface porosity. For best results apply with a low-pressure garden sprayer or airless paint sprayer. Flood coat the surface liberally beginning at the top and allow Aqua Seal to run down the surface about 8 inches. For best sealing results, back roll the wet coat to uniformly force the sealer into the surface.

Check out our ["GenStone Products Use & Care"](https://go.genstone.com/use-and-care-guide-PDF) guide to get more detailed information on the best maintenance practices.

#### How do I measure the area to be covered in order to purchase the right amount of stone veneer?

- Article ID: 360000675508
- Status: Published
- Visibility: Public
- Locale: en-us

When you are trying to determine how many GenStone panels and accessories you need, there are two ways to go about it. One way is to start by measuring the total linear feet in your improvement area. A linear foot is simply a measurement of how many feet make up the area horizontally. 

The other way is to determine how many square feet your area is composed of and divide that number by the coverage area of the **[stacked stone](https://genstone.com/faux-stone-panels/stacked-stone)** or **[brick veneer](https://genstone.com/faux-stone-panels/brick-veneer)** panel. We typically recommend using the linear foot method because you will need the linear feet measurement to figure out how many ledgers and trim you may want to incorporate into the project. 

Here’s how you would determine how many stacked stone panels and ledgers you would need for this project. For a more in-depth look at a project like this, **[download the guide linked here](https://go.genstone.com/how-much-do-i-need-measuringhelp)** to learn how to measure for corner panels, corner ledgers, and more.

#### Formulas for Stacked Stone and Brick

**Example Project:** 65' Long by 4' Tall Wainscot

- 65 x 12 = **780 inches long**

- Each panel provides 41″ of width coverage so 780 / 41 = **19.02 panels**

Each panel delivers 11″ of height coverage so 19.02 x 4 = **76.08 panels**

- Always add 5% and round up! 76.08 x 1.05 = **80 total panels needed**

- Each ledger provides 42″ of width and 3.5″ of height

780 / 42 = **18.57 ledgers**

- Always add 5% and round up! 18.57 x 1.05 = **20 total ledgers needed**

When figure the materials needed for a project, you would need to take a measurement of the height and the length of the wall you intend to cover.  Divide the height of the wall by the height of your panel and the length of the wall by the length of the panel. Multiplying these numbers together will give the number of full panels need for the area. For trim and ledger, measure your linear footage and divide by 4.  This will be the number of pieces needed.

## Glossary

New to the improvement world? These terms and definitions will help bring you up to speed.

### Siding Terms

Many of our customers are new to remodeling which is ok because GenStone is so easy to install anyone with the ability to use a hand saw and screwdriver can do it. We’ve found that getting in tune with the common language used in our industry can be the most difficult part of a GenStone project. Therefore, we’ve put together a short list of commonly used terms so you can get up to speed with what you may have heard or read while researching your siding project.

#### Vocabulary for the Siding Industry

- Article ID: 208034747
- Status: Published
- Visibility: Public
- Locale: en-us
- Updated: 2015-08-20T23:42:15Z
- Zendesk URL: https://support.genstone.com/hc/en-us/articles/208034747

**Panel Siding**


Siding that comes in sections, usually four foot by eight foot.


**Aluminum Siding**


Metal siding made to look like wooden lap siding.


**Brick Veneer**


A wall construction method in which a layer of brick is attached to the framework or a structure using brick ties.


**Cast Stone (boooo!)**


Man-made material fashioned to resemble coral stone or some other natural stone.


**Caulking**


A waterproof material used to seal joints.


**Channel**


The area of the accessory trim or corner post where siding or soffit panels are inserted.


**Fiberboard Siding**


Siding made of composite wood material.


**General Contractor**


A construction professional who enters into a contract with the owner of a project  for the construction of the project.


**Lap Siding**


Siding that looks like individual boards. Each siding piece is “lapped” over an adjoining piece below for waterproofing.


**Mason**


A professional who builds or works with brick or stone.


**Mortar**


Any of various bonding materials used in masonry especially a plastic mixture of cement or lime and sand and water that hardens into place and bonds brick or stone.


**Shipping Broker**


Specializes in the coordination of transportation services.


**Shake Siding**


Also known as shingle siding. Comes in widths of four to twelve inches.


**Square Feet**


A unit of measurement used to denote the amount of siding material used for a particular siding job including material waste.


**Veneer**


Is one ply or a thickness of something.


**Vinyl Siding**


Siding made of poly vinyl chloride.
